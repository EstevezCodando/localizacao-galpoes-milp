\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Guide rather than prune heuristically.}
Under capacity coupling, the information a ranking carries is better spent deciding \emph{where
to search} than \emph{what to remove}. Heuristic pruning by a ranking is irreversible and has to keep slack for every
capacity constraint (safe exclusion by reduced costs, which we use, is a different matter); guidance keeps the full domain reachable and only changes the order in which
the search visits it. This matches the trend from learned pruning towards trust regions
\citep{han2023}, learned neighborhoods \citep{huang2023} and learned delegation
\citep{li2021delegate}. The closed test supports the first half of this statement and qualifies the second: guidance
by a ranking roughly halved the primal integral of the full solver, but the ranking that worked
was the one read from the LP relaxation, not the learned one.

\paragraph{Where the gain came from.}
In the closed test the gain over the full solver was obtained by the restriction mechanisms with
a learning-free ranking, and replacing that ranking by the learned one made the result worse. We
can list explanations but cannot discriminate among them with the present data: the strong LP
may already carry most of the information the GNN could learn about which facilities to open,
and it is computed for the instance at hand rather than for a training distribution; the training
set is small and covers one size and two spatial families; the target (frequency in a pool of
solutions) may be misaligned with what the expansion needs; and the learned score has an
inference cost that the LP score does not. The result therefore concerns this ranker under this
training budget. For subproblem selection the evidence remains exploratory, since the learned and
GNN-guided selectors were evaluated only in the pilots: they did not separate from rotation
there, and the repetition rate we measured (54--58\% without memory) is an inexpensive diagnostic
that we suggest reporting for any LNS before investing in a learned selector.

\paragraph{The classical counterpart is the baseline that matters.}
In every line of work we reconstructed, a classical rule with the same function captured most of
the gain: the LP ranking for pruning, the add-gain filter for swaps, Mettu--Plaxton with local
search for uniform facility location, and a linear continuous approximation for location-routing
(Appendix~\ref{app:reconstructions}). Reporting a learned method only against the raw solver
overstates its contribution.

\paragraph{Low prediction error is not decision quality.}
A routing-cost surrogate with 7.5\% mean absolute percentage error ranked competing
configurations poorly (Kendall $\tau=0.34$), and the optimizer gravitated to configurations where
the surrogate was relatively optimistic.

\paragraph{Timing is the fragile part.}
Two of the errors found by the audit of our own harness affected only timing metrics, yet would
have changed the comparison of anytime behavior. Pinning cores, limiting threads and recording CPU
time next to wall time are cheap and make contention visible.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Most instances are synthetic. Holmberg provides proven optima but small sizes, and the
Olist case relies on assumed capacities and fixed costs.
\item Outside Holmberg, quality is measured against the best known solution.
\item All experiments use one machine with three concurrent pinned runs.
\item The reconstructions in the appendix are methodological reconstructions, not reproductions
of the original code; their limitations are those of our implementations, not of the original
articles.
\item The contribution of clustering itself is not isolated: the comparison with the naive
decomposition shows the value of coordination in this construction, not of grouping by cost
profile against, e.g., random groups of the same sizes.
\item In the pilots, the GNN-guided selector of the stand-alone CLNS computed its scores before
the search clock started, so its trajectory is shifted by the inference time. The shift is small
(the recorded overrun of those runs has median 0 and maximum 0.48~s), the method was not part of
the closed test, and the code will be corrected before that method is run again.
\item The final deviation of the pilots was first computed from the returned objective; recomputed
at $T$, three of the 944 pilot runs change and one table entry moves from 1.82\% to 1.84\%.
\item The closed test was run on a machine that was not idle during two of its five sets; 16.5\%
of the runs carry a contention flag. The conclusions on the primal integral are unchanged without
the flagged runs, but the absolute values of the corridor and scale sets are upper estimates, and
those two sets should be replicated on an idle machine.
\item Two of the four hypotheses on the secondary metric have corrected $p$-values of 0.0495 (both). We do not build conclusions on them.
\item The GNN was trained on one instance size and two spatial families. The negative result on
learning concerns this ranker and this training budget (about four CPU-hours of labels), not
learned ranking in general.
\item Kernel search was implemented without the bucket-opening constraint and the objective
cutoff of the original.
\end{itemize}
